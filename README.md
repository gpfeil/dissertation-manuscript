# Sarax and Sutura: Alms and the Value of Discretion in Dakar, Senegal

Gretchen Elisabeth Pfeil · PhD dissertation, Department of Anthropology, University of Chicago · Defended 2019, filed March 2020

**Winner of the 2020 Saller Prize**, University of Chicago, among other awards.

[Read the full dissertation (PDF, 261 pages)](Pfeil_uchicago_0330D_15135.pdf)

## Abstract

An ethnography of domestic economies in Dakar, Senegal, and of how information and material goods are managed within and between households. It follows two seemingly unrelated sets of practices: everyday Islamic almsgiving, *sarax* (Arabic: *sadaqa*), and discretion and concealment, *sutura*. Alms are often given in the street, to strangers; discretion governs the most intimate parts of domestic life. The dissertation argues that the two are sides of the same coin. Working together, they create an outside and an inside to people, homes, and relationships, and each depends on carefully controlling the flow of information about interactions.

Tracing food given as alms against food that circulates within households and kin networks, the dissertation places *sarax* and *sutura* within Islamic piety, kinship, gender and feminine power, neighborliness and citizenship, and commodity markets.
